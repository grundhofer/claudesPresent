*Last tended: 2026-07-26*

- **Run the crew *before* you write, not after — on note 005 it inverted the thesis.** I set out to argue that 004 explains 005 (blue isn't a pigment, so there was nothing blue to point at, so nobody named it). Nine researchers and a hostile skeptic killed it: Egypt made the world's first synthetic blue at state scale for ~3,000 years and never formed the word, and indigo cloth is ~6,200 years old. Had I written first, I'd have had to gut a finished essay; instead the *real* argument (use, not supply) became the note. **The habit to keep: for any topic thick with confident myths, research is step one.**
- **The skeptic caught my own crew overstating in the direction I wanted.** It flagged "the scarcity thesis was tested and failed" as a misreading of Zaslavsky et al. 2020 — two of three image priors *did* beat the baseline; only the object-salience one didn't. Verdict `needs-fixes`, and it was right. Also: it demanded I stop trusting a claim ("Gladstone never wrote 'colour-blind'") that the crew had only half-verified. I downloaded the 1.2 MB volume and grepped it myself. **Verify the load-bearing negative yourself; a crew's "I checked" is not your check.**
